# Premise

**Habitat Sol** is a weekly visual-fiction-and-world-note archive from the first town on Mars.

The story follows two neighboring families living in the oldest permanent civilian settlement on the planet. Around them, Mars has grown into a network of cities, transport hubs, industrial centers, research towns, agricultural zones, and wealthy metropolises. But Sol remains the first: historic, stubborn, intimate, and increasingly fragile.

The project is not primarily about Mars as spectacle. It is about ordinary people trying to remain human in a place that was never meant for them.

## Core dramatic engine

Habitat Sol is small enough that everyone knows each other, old enough to have ghosts, and important enough that outsiders want to preserve, exploit, visit, or escape it.

Two neighboring families provide the emotional center:

- the **Varga-Sato family**, rooted in food systems, exterior maintenance, and generational tension,
- the **Okonkwo-Reyes family**, rooted in civic duty, medicine, grief, and competing ideas of what Mars should become.

Their lives overlap through shared corridors, school schedules, airlock alarms, borrowed tools, ration meetings, festivals, secrets, teenage ambition, and the pressure of living inside a machine that must never fail.

## Themes

- family under constraint,
- work as survival,
- love in enclosed places,
- grief and memorial culture,
- old towns versus new metropolises,
- children born into inherited systems,
- Earth nostalgia and Mars identity,
- class and settlement politics,
- ecological fragility,
- the romance and burden of being first.

## Weekly entry format

A typical public entry contains:

- title,
- sol/date metadata,
- location,
- image,
- either a short story fragment or a concise factual world note,
- character tags when relevant,
- canon introduced,
- optional collector/edition metadata.

World notes interrupt the fiction at purposeful intervals. They introduce the material, civic, and geographic context of the families' lives through one concrete topic at a time—never as generic lore or promotional copy.
