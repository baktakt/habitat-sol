# Albedo Protons as a Probe of Near-Surface Martian Water

**Evidence label:** Established analysis of Mars Science Laboratory measurements plus simulation-based detector sensitivity; operational resource prospecting remains plausible extrapolation
**Last checked:** 2026-11-06
**Author:** Habitat Sol research scan
**Scope:** What a 2026 JGR: Planets study establishes about detecting water-equivalent hydrogen in the upper centimetres of Martian regolith with upward-moving protons.

## Short answer

Mars Science Laboratory's Radiation Assessment Detector can isolate upward-moving “albedo” protons produced by radiation interactions in the ground, providing sensitivity to roughly 1–6 cm depth. Existing RAD data did not resolve the small 2%–7% variations measured along Curiosity's route, while simulations indicate that larger reservoirs may be detectable over months and that a purpose-built detector could detect much larger changes over days.

## What we know

- **Albedo protons sample a shallow subsurface layer that optical observations can miss.**
  - **Evidence:** Löwe et al. modelled where upward-moving protons detected by MSL/RAD are generated and found sensitivity to water-equivalent hydrogen beneath dust at approximately 1–6 cm depth. Because RAD is on the surface and resolves particle direction, it can distinguish a ground-emergent component that an orbiter cannot measure through the atmosphere in the same way. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** The observable is a radiation signal related to water-equivalent hydrogen, not a direct image of an ice layer or a chemical assay distinguishing ice, adsorbed water, and hydrated minerals.

- **The existing rover data did not resolve the small water variations available for comparison.**
  - **Evidence:** The study compared corrected RAD albedo-proton measurements with Dynamic Albedo of Neutrons observations from the same rover. Water-equivalent-hydrogen variations of 2%–7% among the examined formations were not statistically resolved by RAD. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** This is an explicit non-detection. It should not be recast as a map of water abundance or as proof that those formations are compositionally uniform.

- **Long integration may allow the current instrument to detect a substantially wetter target.**
  - **Evidence:** Supporting transport simulations suggest that larger near-surface reservoirs, such as plausible high-latitude deposits or unusually wet local low-latitude material, could be detectable with MSL/RAD after approximately two to three months of measurement. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** This is simulated sensitivity, not a reservoir detected by this method. The required duration depends on signal statistics, instrument geometry, environmental corrections, and the size of the contrast.

- **A detector designed for proton albedo could improve response time for large changes.**
  - **Evidence:** The authors' detector simulation found that a purpose-built instrument could detect a change of about 20% water content in roughly 6–16 days and changes greater than 40% within a few days, with statistical significance. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** These values describe changes or contrasts under the model assumptions; they are not universal minimum water abundances. Instrument design, calibration, terrain, atmospheric conditions, and cosmic-ray variability remain consequential.

- **The analysis explicitly corrects radiation signals that are not caused by ground water.**
  - **Evidence:** The method separates upward protons, estimates alpha-particle contamination, and corrects for solar modulation and long-term atmospheric effects. Reported uncertainty in the corrected albedo-proton series includes counting statistics and contamination estimates. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** Residual systematic uncertainty remains. The paper reports estimated alpha contamination and a mean uncertainty in the corrected flux, underscoring that this is not a simple one-reading prospecting sensor.

## Constraints and uncertainties

- Water-equivalent hydrogen does not uniquely identify accessible water ice. Ground truth by drilling, sampling, or complementary spectroscopy/neutron measurements would still be needed.
- The sensitive depth is centimetres, not the metre-scale excavation depth relevant to many resource systems. A shallow signal may not represent the volume or continuity of a deeper deposit.
- Detectability depends on contrast and integration time. Small variations may remain invisible even when water is present.
- The current result is based on Curiosity/RAD data and numerical particle-transport simulations; the proposed dedicated instrument has not produced flight data on Mars.
- Prospecting a water-rich area also raises landing-safety, scientific-preservation, and planetary-protection constraints; detection alone does not grant operational access.

## Operational implications

- **Established inference:** A settlement survey cannot treat “water detected” as a single class of evidence. Depth sensitivity, chemical form, spatial resolution, statistical confidence, and ground truth must accompany the result.
- **Plausible extrapolation:** A mobile survey programme could combine proton-albedo sensing with neutron data, radar, thermal observations, and selective drilling to screen shallow targets before committing excavation hardware.
- **Detail to avoid or qualify:** Do not claim that Curiosity found a new ice deposit with albedo protons, that 2%–7% water differences were detected, or that a few days of measurement would characterize ordinary low-contrast regolith.

## Habitat Sol canon implications

- This method does not require a canon change. It supports slow, layered resource surveying in which remote or passive indications are followed by local verification before infrastructure decisions.
