# Research Method and Canon Rules

## Purpose

This folder protects both halves of science fiction: research should be traceable, while canon should remain free to make intentional, legible departures from reality.

## Source hierarchy

Use these in roughly this order:

1. Peer-reviewed research, conference papers, and systematic reviews.
2. Mission agencies and technical bodies: NASA, ESA, JAXA, DLR, CSA, CNSA publications where accessible, NIST, ISO, NASA standards.
3. University or government research programme reports.
4. Reputable technical books and expert explainers that link their sources.
5. Journalism only as a lead to the underlying work.

Avoid treating press releases, vendor claims, unsourced social posts, or popular summaries as final evidence.

## Evidence labels

Every substantive note should use one label:

- **Established:** observations, measurements, demonstrated engineering, or strong consensus.
- **Plausible extrapolation:** an inference compatible with evidence but not demonstrated at the required Martian scale or duration.
- **Fictional decision:** Habitat Sol canon overrides or extends a constraint.

A single note may contain all three, but they must remain visually distinct.

## Canon override record

When fiction departs from the research, add an entry here or link to a canon note:

```md
### [Canon change]

**Real-world baseline:** [linked evidence]
**Habitat Sol decision:** [what is different]
**Why it exists:** story, theme, or practical reason
**Consequences we must preserve:** [limits, costs, effects on culture/technology]
**Status:** proposed | accepted | superseded
```

This prevents a convenient invention from silently removing every dramatic consequence.

## Citation practice

Use a stable URL, DOI, report identifier, or archived copy where possible. For a numerical claim, write the number, unit, conditions, and source in the same paragraph. Record the access date for web sources.
