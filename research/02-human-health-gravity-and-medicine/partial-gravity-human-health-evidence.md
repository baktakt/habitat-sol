# Partial Gravity and Long-Duration Human Health

**Evidence label:** Established evidence about microgravity and short-duration/analog partial-gravity research; long-duration Mars-health outcome remains uncertain
**Last checked:** 2026-08-28
**Author:** Habitat Sol research scan
**Scope:** What current evidence supports—and does not support—about whether Mars’s approximately 0.38 g environment can maintain human health during long residence.

## Short answer

Mars gravity should not be treated as a demonstrated health countermeasure. NASA’s 2021 synthesis, drawing on analog and microgravity evidence, finds no direct long-duration human partial-gravity data and judges gravity below 0.4 g likely insufficient to maintain musculoskeletal and cardiopulmonary conditioning over the long term. A 2025 parabolic-flight cell experiment found altered immune-cell/endothelium interactions at 0.38 g, but it tested short exposures in a laboratory system rather than astronaut health.

## What we know

- **Long-duration human evidence at Mars gravity does not yet exist.**
  - **Evidence:** NASA’s 2021 technical paper states directly that long-term partial-gravity effects on humans lack direct evidence. It synthesizes microgravity, simulated-gravity, and partial-gravity literature rather than a Mars-gravity residence study. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** This means neither a safe duration nor a gravity threshold that protects every physiological system has been established for people living at 0.38 g.

- **The best available synthesis flags 0.38 g as unlikely to preserve all musculoskeletal and cardiopulmonary conditioning without additional measures.**
  - **Evidence:** The NASA paper reports that a systematic review of partial gravity from 0.1 to 0.4 g found exposures below 0.4 g appeared insufficient to maintain musculoskeletal and cardiopulmonary properties in the long term. The same source says exercise and resistance training are useful countermeasures but do not preserve all systems to an Earth-gravity standard. [1][2]
  - **Conditions / limits:** “Likely insufficient” is an evidence-based assessment, not a measurement of bone loss, strength loss, or clinical events after a particular number of Martian days. The source does not establish that a Mars settlement requires artificial gravity.

- **A recent experiment extends the concern to a cellular immune-related mechanism, not to clinical disease.**
  - **Evidence:** In parabolic flight, Du et al. exposed THP-1 immune cells and human endothelial-cell layers to lunar (0.16 g) and Martian (0.38 g) phases. At partial gravity, the experiment found increased immune-cell floating speed, lower adhesion to endothelial cells, altered adhesion-marker expression, and changes in endothelial F-actin organization; the authors say the effect magnitudes by gravity level need further study. [3]
  - **Conditions / limits:** Parabolic-flight partial-gravity intervals are brief, and the study used cell cultures in a flow chamber. It cannot predict infection rates, wound healing, inflammatory disease, medication needs, or cumulative immune effects in a Mars crew.

## Constraints and uncertainties

- ISS and other long-duration spaceflight evidence is primarily microgravity evidence. It establishes that physiological protection needs active management in spaceflight, but it cannot quantify the benefit of 0.38 g.
- Different systems may have different gravity-response thresholds. A gravity level that improves one measure cannot be assumed to protect bone, muscle, cardiovascular conditioning, vision, immunity, reproduction, or development equally.
- Radiation, confinement, sleep disruption, workload, nutrition, and the journey to and from Mars interact with gravity-related risk. The cited sources do not resolve these combined long-duration effects.

## Writing implications

- **Established inference:** Medical monitoring, exercise, rehabilitation capacity, and conservative task design remain credible routine infrastructure on Mars; ordinary walking at 0.38 g is not evidence that residents are physiologically Earth-normal.
- **Plausible extrapolation:** A mature settlement may schedule high-load work, post-arrival rehabilitation, and return-to-Earth preparation around individual conditioning data rather than a universal “Mars-adapted” status.
- **Detail to avoid or qualify:** Avoid stating that Mars gravity has been proven sufficient to prevent long-term deconditioning, or that one short partial-gravity experiment proves a specific illness in settlers.

## Habitat Sol canon implications

- No Habitat Sol canon decision on artificial gravity, long-term health thresholds, or partial-gravity countermeasure requirements was found in the research library.
- If canon treats multi-year 0.38 g residence as broadly healthy, record the departure explicitly and preserve its consequences: screening, exercise and rehabilitation norms, medical capacity, or a defined technological countermeasure.
