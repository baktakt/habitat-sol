# Habitat Sol — Science Reference Library

This directory is the writers’ evidence base for life on Mars. It records real, attributable research and turns it into practical story constraints, opportunities, and carefully labelled extrapolations.

> **Relationship to `writers-input/`:** `writers-input/` is the creative sandbox: ideas, character notes, scenes, story arcs, and unresolved questions. This directory is the reference library. Writers may begin in either place, but scientific claims used to resolve a question should be captured here with a source.

## How to use this library

1. Start with the topic folder closest to your writing question.
2. Prefer its `README.md` for an at-a-glance briefing and links to detailed notes.
3. Treat statements according to their evidence label:
   - **Established** — directly supported by current research, data, or operational experience.
   - **Plausible extrapolation** — a reasoned extension that current science does not yet prove.
   - **Fictional decision** — deliberately invented canon. Preserve its internal consequences.
4. If a story needs to bend reality, write the fiction down explicitly—what changes, what it enables, and which real constraint it bypasses.

## Standards for research notes

- Cite primary sources where possible: peer-reviewed studies, NASA/ESA/JAXA reports, mission documentation, standards, or technical agency publications.
- Add the source URL or DOI and access date; quote numerical assumptions with units.
- Separate evidence from interpretation and from story use.
- Do not make a source sound more certain than it is. Note uncertainty, scale, and Earth-to-Mars limitations.
- Research describes the current scientific picture; it does **not** automatically overrule established Habitat Sol canon.

## Suggested note format

Create focused Markdown notes using this structure:

```md
# Topic title

**Evidence label:** Established | Plausible extrapolation | Fictional decision
**Last checked:** YYYY-MM-DD
**Scope:** One-sentence description.

## What we know
- Claim with units, conditions, and a citation.

## Constraints and uncertainties
- What would change the answer; limitations of the evidence.

## Writing implications
- Concrete consequences for daily life, conflict, plot, or setting.

## Sources
1. Organisation/author, *title* (year). URL or DOI. Accessed YYYY-MM-DD.
```

## Initial priority questions

The library should answer the questions most likely to make scenes feel real:

- What fails first in a habitat, and how much time do people have?
- What is abundant, scarce, recycled, imported, or impossible to replace?
- How do people eat, sleep, exercise, clean themselves, and raise children under Martian constraints?
- How far and how fast can people or cargo travel in realistic conditions?
- What does radiation, dust, partial gravity, and communication delay do to work and relationships?
- What assumptions are intentionally different in Habitat Sol’s future, and what secondary consequences follow?
