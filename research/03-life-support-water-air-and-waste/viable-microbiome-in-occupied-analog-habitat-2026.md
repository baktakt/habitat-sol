# Viable Microbiome in an Occupied Lunar–Mars Analog Habitat

**Evidence label:** Established Earth-analog surface-sampling evidence; implications for a long-duration Mars habitat remain plausible extrapolation
**Last checked:** 2026-10-02
**Author:** Habitat Sol research scan
**Scope:** What a newly published 21-day occupation study establishes about viable microorganisms, spatial variation, and monitoring limits inside the Integrated Lunar-Mars Analog Habitat (ILMAH).

## Short answer

A 21-day continuously occupied Earth analog developed strongly location-dependent viable microbial communities: bacterial burden was higher in high-traffic entrance, kitchen, bathroom, and crew-quarter modules than in lower-traffic areas, while viable fungal burden did not differ significantly by location. Culture plates counted 100–1,000 times fewer organisms than viability-targeted molecular assays, so culture alone would have missed much of the detected living community. This supports zone-specific biosurveillance and cleaning, but it does not predict disease or the microbiome of a multi-year Mars settlement.

## What we know

- **Viable bacterial burden varied strongly by how spaces were used.**
  - **Evidence:** During the 21-day continuous-occupation trial, propidium-monoazide digital PCR (PMA-dPCR) measured viable bacterial loads from approximately 10^8 to 10^11 16S rRNA gene copies per square metre. Burdens in the entrance, kitchen, bathroom, and crew quarters were 4.11 log10 higher than in the study's low-traffic module group (Welch's test, p < 0.001); community composition also differed by location (PERMANOVA p = 0.021). [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** Gene-copy abundance is not a count of individual organisms, and the result belongs to this habitat, occupants, cleaning regime, sampling design, and 21-day period. It is not a universal loading threshold for spacecraft or settlements.

- **Fungi and bacteria did not follow the same spatial pattern.**
  - **Evidence:** Viable fungal loads were more spatially uniform and did not differ significantly among sampled locations (p = 0.38), whereas bacterial loads and community structure showed clear location effects. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** A non-significant location test does not prove that fungal burden is constant over longer periods, under different humidity conditions, or behind inaccessible panels and equipment.

- **Routine culture substantially underrepresented the viable microbial signal.**
  - **Evidence:** Counts on ready-to-use dry-sheet culture media were 2–3 orders of magnitude below PMA-dPCR results. Intracellular ATP correlated with PMA-dPCR across modules, and the authors interpret the gap as evidence of a substantial viable-but-nonculturable fraction. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** PMA treatment, ATP assays, culture, amplicon sequencing, and shotgun metagenomics measure different properties and have different biases. None alone is a complete census of metabolic activity, exposure, or clinical hazard.

- **Low abundance risk-associated taxa and functional signatures were detectable without a reported outbreak.**
  - **Evidence:** The study characterized 240 cultured isolates—140 bacterial and 100 fungal—and identified 42 bacterial and 26 fungal species. Shotgun metagenomics detected low-abundance viable biosafety-level-2 taxa, including *Salmonella enterica* and *Serratia* species, while antimicrobial-resistance genes and virulence-factor signatures were concentrated in high-traffic modules and were largely carried by *Pseudomonas*. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** Taxonomic detection or a gene signature does not establish expression, transmission, infection, antibiotic failure, or disease. The study sampled habitat surfaces rather than reporting clinical outcomes in occupants.

## Constraints and uncertainties

- ILMAH is an Earth analog. The study did not reproduce Martian gravity, radiation, external atmosphere, dust chemistry, or multi-year isolation, and it cannot establish how those factors would reshape microbial ecology.
- Twenty-one days is short relative to a transit or settlement lifetime. Long-term biofilm development, succession, repeated disinfection, material degradation, closed-loop water interactions, and microbial evolution remain unresolved.
- The reported spatial pattern may depend on module design, traffic, humidity, surface materials, cleaning, and occupant behaviour. It should guide monitoring hypotheses, not define fixed Mars values.
- Molecular detection needs operational interpretation: distinguishing harmless residents, material-damaging organisms, food or water contamination, and actual health threats requires additional sampling and context.

## Operational implications

- **Established inference:** Sampling plans should treat entrances, kitchens, bathrooms, and crew quarters as distinct operational zones; a habitat-wide average can hide local concentrations.
- **Established inference:** Culture remains useful for isolate testing, but culture alone is an incomplete biosurveillance method. Combining viability-targeted molecular assays, ATP or other rapid screening, and targeted culture provides different kinds of evidence.
- **Plausible extrapolation:** A mature Mars habitat would likely trend microbial measurements over time and trigger targeted inspection, cleaning, airflow or moisture checks, and follow-up identification when a module departs from its own baseline.
- **Detail to avoid or qualify:** Do not turn detection of a biosafety-level-2 taxon or resistance gene into a diagnosed infection, or claim that this 21-day analog proves a specific Mars cleaning schedule.

## Habitat Sol canon implications

- No canon change is required. The finding supports ordinary contamination-control labour, differentiated clean/dirty zones, and routine environmental sampling without implying that inhabited spaces are visibly dirty or unsafe.
- If Habitat Sol uses rapid microbial monitors, retain confirmatory testing and trend interpretation; a single positive molecular signal should not automatically mean an outbreak.
