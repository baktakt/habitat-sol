# Low-Pressure Plasma CO₂ Conversion for Mars ISRU

**Evidence label:** Established laboratory plasma evidence; a practical Mars oxygen or chemical-production system remains plausible extrapolation
**Last checked:** 2026-10-02
**Author:** Habitat Sol research scan
**Scope:** What a newly published radio-frequency inductively coupled plasma experiment establishes about low-pressure carbon-dioxide conversion and what it does not yet establish for Mars in-situ resource utilization (ISRU).

## Short answer

A 13.56 MHz radio-frequency inductively coupled plasma converted carbon dioxide across 5–40 Pa laboratory tests, reaching a reported maximum CO₂ conversion of 37.1%; an optimized three-turn planar coil reached a peak electrical power-transfer efficiency of 66.9%. The experiment and its fluid-chemistry model show that pressure changes both plasma performance and the dominant dissociation pathway. These are reactor-physics results—not demonstrated oxygen-production efficiency, Mars-atmosphere operation, or a complete ISRU plant.

## What we know

- **The laboratory reactor converted a measurable fraction of CO₂ at low pressure.**
  - **Evidence:** Jiang et al. operated a 13.56 MHz radio-frequency inductively coupled plasma (RF-ICP) from 5 to 40 Pa and varied coil turns and applied power. The optimized three-turn planar coil achieved a reported maximum CO₂ conversion of 37.1%. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** “CO₂ conversion” is the fraction of feed carbon dioxide transformed under the tested reactor conditions. It is not by itself the yield or purity of collectable oxygen, an energy-efficiency figure, or evidence of continuous production from unprocessed Martian atmosphere.

- **Electrical coupling and chemical conversion are separate performance measures.**
  - **Evidence:** Voltage-current measurements gave a peak power-transfer efficiency of 66.9% for the optimized coil. The paper reports that value separately from the maximum 37.1% CO₂ conversion. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** Power-transfer efficiency describes electrical power coupled into the plasma; it does not include atmospheric collection and compression, pumps, gas separation, oxygen purification and storage, reactor cooling, controls, or maintenance. The two reported maxima should not be assumed to occur at one operating point unless a design specifies it.

- **Pressure produced a non-monotonic operating window.**
  - **Evidence:** Langmuir-probe diagnostics and optical-emission spectroscopy showed electron density peaking at approximately 10–15 Pa. At lower pressure, reduced collisionality limited electron-impact reaction rates; at higher pressure, falling electron temperature and density suppressed electron-impact dissociation and ionization. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** This trend belongs to the reported geometry, frequency, power range, feed conditions, and diagnostics. It is not a universal optimum for every plasma reactor.

- **The dominant modeled dissociation route changed with pressure.**
  - **Evidence:** A two-dimensional fluid-chemistry model reproduced the observed conversion trend. It attributed a strong role to an ionization–recombination pathway at or below 15 Pa, while direct electron-impact dissociation became dominant above 15 Pa as the ionization pathway weakened. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** Agreement with the measured conversion trend supports the mechanism but does not validate every modeled reaction rate, by-product concentration, electrode or wall effect, or long-duration behaviour.

## Constraints and uncertainties

- The authors describe the work as a basis for future extension and validation at higher pressures relevant to Mars ISRU. The tested 5–40 Pa regime should not be presented as a field demonstration using ambient Martian gas.
- The reported abstract does not close the product-separation, oxygen purity, carbon-monoxide handling, energy-per-unit-product, thermal-management, throughput, durability, dust-tolerance, or autonomous-control budgets needed for an operational plant.
- A maximum conversion fraction does not establish useful production rate. Reactor volume, gas flow, residence time, duty cycle, and downstream recovery determine how much usable product a system delivers.
- A settlement-scale assessment must compare plasma conversion with alternatives such as solid-oxide electrolysis on common inputs and boundaries; this paper alone does not show that RF-ICP is the preferred Mars architecture.

## Operational implications

- **Established inference:** Pressure control is part of the reaction system rather than merely a gas-storage detail; compressors, valves, pumps, and diagnostics affect both conversion and dominant chemistry.
- **Established inference:** Reactor status cannot be represented by one efficiency number. Electrical coupling, feed conversion, product selectivity, separation efficiency, throughput, and total plant energy must be tracked separately.
- **Plausible extrapolation:** If a future settlement used plasma CO₂ conversion, operators would likely manage a narrow qualified pressure/power envelope and monitor drift in electron-state or optical signals as part of preventive maintenance.
- **Detail to avoid or qualify:** Do not describe the 37.1% conversion as 37.1% oxygen yield, the 66.9% power-transfer value as total plant efficiency, or the experiment as proof that oxygen can be produced directly from ambient Mars air without compression and separation equipment.

## Habitat Sol canon implications

- No canon change is required. The work supports treating atmospheric CO₂ as potentially useful feedstock while preserving compressors, power electronics, gas cleanup, separation, heat rejection, maintenance, and storage as visible industrial infrastructure.
- Any canon claim about settlement-scale oxygen or fuel autonomy still needs an explicit plant architecture and material-and-energy budget.
