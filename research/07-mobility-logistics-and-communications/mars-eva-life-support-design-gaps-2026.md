# Mars EVA Life-Support Design Gaps

**Evidence label:** Established current NASA design-study evidence; operational consequences are clearly marked as plausible extrapolation

**Last checked:** 2026-08-21

**Author:** Habitat Sol research scan

**Scope:** What NASA's July 2026 Mars portable-life-support-system (PLSS) planning identifies as environmental and engineering differences between a Mars EVA suit and existing lunar/low-Earth-orbit suit approaches.

## Short answer

A Mars EVA suit is not a straightforward reuse of an ISS or lunar suit. NASA's current Mars PLSS planning identifies the thin but non-vacuum CO₂ atmosphere, airborne dust, cold temperatures, 0.38 g, and long mission profile as design drivers that can invalidate or alter components used in present suit systems. This is a development roadmap and trade study, not a demonstrated Mars suit.

## What we know

- **Mars environmental conditions affect the suit's external heat-rejection and insulation strategy.**
  - **Evidence:** The NASA team gives a Mars pressure range of 0.2–9 torr, reports temperatures from about −143 °C to 20 °C depending on site, season, and time of day, and says that multilayer insulation (MLI) does not provide adequate insulation in the Martian atmospheric regime considered. It identifies thicker conventional insulation or aerogels as candidates requiring testing, with expected mobility effects. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** These are design-study inputs and conclusions for the proposed MxEMU/PLSS, not field performance measurements on Mars; local pressure and temperature vary.

- **Several familiar PLSS functions require redesign or a different operating mode on Mars.**
  - **Evidence:** The presentation says that rapid-cycle-amine CO₂ swing beds do not operate as-is in a CO₂ atmosphere and are being investigated with sweep gas; the spacesuit water-membrane evaporator is expected to lose performance at Martian pressures up to 10 torr and may also need sweep gas. Filters are required to limit dust intrusion while retaining the evaporator flow path. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** “Expected” performance and candidate technologies are not a flight-qualified solution. The report lists lithium hydroxide, ionic-liquid sorbents, hollow-fibre modules, and other options as technologies still being developed or evaluated.

- **NASA treats early Mars EVA capability as a bounded, still-maturing system.**
  - **Evidence:** For two short-stay reference mission cases, the report lists a preliminary requirement of 25 EVAs. It states that the prior xEMU reference design took roughly 15 years to develop and anticipates a similar development duration for a Mars suit, beginning with a breadboard test bed and then a design-verification-and-test unit. [1]
  - **Conditions / limits:** The mission cases and EVA count are planning assumptions, not a general limit on Mars surface work or a settlement operating rule.

## Constraints and uncertainties

- A suit-system study does not establish how often a real crew can safely perform EVAs, how much maintenance a mature settlement suit fleet needs, or the failure rate of dust filters, seals, and thermal equipment.
- The source explicitly leaves significant trade work unresolved: fault tolerance, suit mass, EVA duration, CO₂ removal, cooling, insulation, dust protection, and whether a Mars suit must also support a deep-space emergency EVA.
- Do not treat the cited 0.2–9 torr, −143 °C to 20 °C, or 25-EVA figures as universal site-independent Mars values or as Habitat Sol canon.

## Writing implications

- **Established inference:** Surface work depends on a suit-plus-support system—thermal control, CO₂ removal, oxygen, water, sensors, communications, and dust exclusion—not merely a pressure garment and oxygen tank.
- **Plausible extrapolation:** EVA preparation, post-EVA dust inspection, filter/service work, and thermal-system troubleshooting can credibly consume a meaningful share of surface-operations labour, especially after extreme weather or long traverses.
- **Detail to avoid or qualify:** Avoid portraying an EVA suit as interchangeable with a vacuum-only orbital suit, or assuming that a technology demonstrated in hard vacuum works unchanged in Mars's low-pressure CO₂ atmosphere.

## Habitat Sol canon implications

- No Habitat Sol canon decision was found in the research library for EVA duration, suit architecture, or suit-maintenance cadence.
- If canon needs routine long-range EVA, record what provides the additional reliability: more suits and replaceable modules, rover/umbilical support, short work shifts, local service infrastructure, or an explicit fictional advance. Preserve the resulting labour, spares, and rescue constraints.
