# The Tomatoes Failed During Breakfast

Mira Varga noticed the leaves before the alarms did.

The youngest tomato plants had curled inward overnight, each one holding its thirst like a fist. She stood between the rows with her breakfast cooling in one hand and her thumb pressed against the moisture reader in the other, waiting for the number to become less impossible.

It did not.

Behind her, on the family channel, Kenji was trying to make Tomas laugh by balancing a squeeze-packet of miso on his nose. Tomas laughed too loudly. Lina did not laugh at all. She was watching the departure board again, where the morning shuttle to Meridian had been delayed by dust.

Next door, Amara Okonkwo was already awake, reading the council brief that said Habitat Sol had forty-one days before water restrictions became public.

No one said shortage. Not at breakfast. Not with children listening.

Outside, the sun rose small and cold over the oldest human town on Mars.
