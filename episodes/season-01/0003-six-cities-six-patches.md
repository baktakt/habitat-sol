# The Six Cities

## Slide 1 — The patches of Mars

Before a name is read, a patch can tell you where someone comes from.

Each of Mars’s six early settlements has its own civic badge, worn on work clothes and equipment—and sometimes on private clothing.

They share the same red, navy, and cream frame, but each carries a different symbol at its center.

Together, they tell the story of how six habitats became six very different cities.

## Slide 2 — Habitat Sol

Mars did not begin with cities. It began with places like Sol: the first civilian town, now home to around 2,000 people.

Its round patch shelters a small sprout beneath a greenhouse arch and an amber sun. It stands for cultivation, collective care, and the fragile continuity that turned a habitat into a community.

The other settlements grew larger. Sol remembers being first.

## Slide 3 — Habitat Meridian

As Mars grew, its routes and decisions converged in Meridian.

Around 250,000 people live in the administrative and transport capital—a city shaped by logistics, law, influence, and control. Its patch reduces that purpose to three route lines meeting at a single meridian point beneath the horizon.

Across Mars, movement passes through Meridian. So does power.

## Slide 4 — Habitat Elysium

Elysium became the future Mars once imagined for itself: clean, fashionable, medically advanced, and very aware of appearances.

With around 600,000 residents, it is the largest and wealthiest of the early cities. Its patch combines a medical bioform, a water droplet, and an orbital arc—symbols of health, biotechnology, refinement, and reach.

In Elysium, progress is visible—and status is meant to be seen.

## Slide 5 — Habitat Ferrum

Every growing world needs somewhere that can build what the others depend on. On Mars, that place is Ferrum.

Its roughly 90,000 residents live in the industrial center of the settlement network, where fabrication, skilled labor, and union solidarity shape civic life. The patch places two structural beams inside a heavy hexagonal frame, above the glow of a furnace.

Ferrum’s identity is simple: make it strong, and make it last.

## Slide 6 — Habitat Astra

Astra was built around questions.

Around 45,000 people live in its universities, research institutions, and the politically restless city around them. Knowledge, argument, discovery, and ambition all belong to its civic character.

Its patch joins an open book to an observatory aperture, with three stars and a measured trajectory arc above them: learn what is known, then look beyond it.

## Slide 7 — Habitat Arcadia

On Mars, food, water, and ice are not background resources. They are the work that makes every other future possible.

Arcadia’s roughly 75,000 residents focus on agriculture, ecology, and long-term self-sufficiency. Its patch brings together a water droplet, an ice crystal, a green leaf, and a terraced basin.

Arcadia is not an escape into nature. It is the careful construction of one.
