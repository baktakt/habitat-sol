# Habitat Sol — Viability Checklist for Writers

**Purpose:** A writer-facing list of the things the universe must deliberately *invent*, decide, or constrain before a town of roughly 2,000 people can plausibly live in Habitat Sol.

This is **not canon yet**. Tick an item only when we have made a fictional decision that includes: how it works, who operates it, what fails, what it costs, and what story pressure it creates.

## Evidence labels

- **Established evidence** — demonstrated or directly measured today.
- **Plausible extrapolation** — possible in principle, but not demonstrated at Habitat Sol scale or on Mars.
- **Fictional invention required** — a major capability the setting must posit beyond current operational reality.

## Working rule

Current research gives us components: closed-loop life-support hardware, plant-growth experiments, robotic Mars resource experiments, and high-reliability spacecraft operations. It does **not** demonstrate a self-sufficient 2,000-person Martian town. Habitat Sol therefore needs explicit answers rather than a magic “Mars colony system.”

---

# Priority 0 — The town must not die this week

These are the first inventions/decisions. Without them, Habitat Sol cannot exist long enough to become a town.

- [ ] **Pressure habitat and breathable atmosphere**  
  **Question to invent:** What keeps 2,000 people supplied with safe cabin pressure, oxygen, nitrogen/argon buffer gas, humidity control, carbon-dioxide removal, trace-contaminant control, and fire safety?  
  **Reality status:** ISS-class environmental-control and life-support systems demonstrate important pieces, but not settlement-scale, multi-generation self-sufficiency.  
  **Writer decisions:** Where does oxygen come from day to day? Is air centrally managed or ring-by-ring? How many hours can a neighborhood survive isolated? What does an air-quality alarm mean socially?

- [ ] **Water source, recovery, storage, and loss control**  
  **Question to invent:** Where is the settlement’s water ice, how is it extracted and purified, and how does the system survive leaks, contamination, drought, or power loss?  
  **Reality status:** Water recovery is operational in orbit; accessible Martian ice and settlement-scale extraction/refining remain an extrapolation.  
  **Writer decisions:** Is Sol built over shallow ice, supplied by an ice mine, or connected to Arcadia? What percentage is recovered? Who sees water-use data? What is a normal household ration? What happens when restrictions become public?

- [ ] **Reliable power through dust, night, winter, and equipment failure**  
  **Question to invent:** What power architecture runs life support continuously when solar output falls and hardware is degraded?  
  **Reality status:** Solar and nuclear technologies exist, but a resilient Martian town-scale grid is not demonstrated. Dust is more dangerous operationally than aerodynamically: it reduces solar output and coats equipment.  
  **Writer decisions:** Solar, fission, storage, imported fuel, or a mix? Who gets power first in shortages? Is there a visible “grid weather” culture? What parts of Sol run on old founding-era equipment?

- [ ] **Radiation protection and storm sheltering**  
  **Question to invent:** How does the town protect residents from chronic galactic cosmic radiation and solar-particle events over decades?  
  **Reality status:** Mars has a thin atmosphere and no global magnetic shield comparable to Earth’s. Shielding, forecasting, and operational limits are necessary; long-term human outcomes remain uncertain.  
  **Writer decisions:** Is Sol buried, bermed with regolith, inside lava-tube-adjacent structure, or protected by water walls? How often do residents use shelters? Does radiation exposure affect careers, pregnancy, insurance, or travel permissions?

- [ ] **Thermal control, dust sealing, and exterior survival**  
  **Question to invent:** How do habitat modules reject heat, survive cold, keep dust out of seals, and maintain airlocks, suits, rovers, radiators, and solar surfaces?  
  **Reality status:** Mars is cold, dusty, and low-pressure. Dust-storm operational risk is real even where wind force is lower than Earth intuition suggests.  
  **Writer decisions:** What does dust season look/smell/sound like indoors? How much exterior work is routine? Which jobs are dangerous? What domestic rituals follow an EVA?

- [ ] **Failure containment and emergency governance**  
  **Question to invent:** How does a small town prevent one leak, fire, crop disease, software fault, or contaminated water loop from becoming a settlement-ending event?  
  **Reality status:** Spacecraft have emergency procedures; town-scale redundancy and civic governance are fictional extrapolations.  
  **Writer decisions:** Pressure compartments, independent rings, emergency drills, authority to seal doors, public warning systems, mutual-aid rules, memorial practices, and the political aftermath of an avoidable failure.

# Priority 1 — The town must survive a missed resupply window

These determine whether Habitat Sol is a settlement or merely a remote outpost with more residents.

- [ ] **Food calories, nutrients, and crop resilience**  
  **Question to invent:** How does Sol feed 2,000 people across normal years, pest outbreaks, water limits, crop failures, and resupply delays?  
  **Reality status:** NASA has demonstrated space plant-growth research, not a complete, closed-loop food system for a town.  
  **Writer decisions:** Which crops provide bulk calories? How much food is grown locally versus imported? Are algae, fungi, cultured meat, insects, or hydroponic staples normal? Who controls seed stock and nutrient recipes? What does a bad harvest do to family life?

- [ ] **Nutrient, fertilizer, and agricultural-input loops**  
  **Question to invent:** Plants need more than water and light. Where do nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, micronutrients, growth media, pollination, pest control, and replacement hardware come from?  
  **Reality status:** This is a major gap in casual Mars fiction. Recycling and some local processing are plausible; fully closed agricultural inputs at settlement scale are unproven.  
  **Writer decisions:** Is Mira’s greenhouse team dependent on imported trace minerals? Is waste recovery civic infrastructure? Can a missing micronutrient become a political crisis?

- [ ] **Waste, sanitation, and material recycling**  
  **Question to invent:** What happens to sewage, food waste, plastics, textiles, metals, filters, hazardous waste, and dead electronics?  
  **Reality status:** Recovery and recycling exist in constrained spacecraft settings, but high-throughput settlement loops require extrapolation.  
  **Writer decisions:** What is discarded versus repaired? Who works waste systems? Is there a social taboo around waste? What objects are treasured because materials are scarce?

- [ ] **Maintenance, spare parts, and local manufacturing**  
  **Question to invent:** How does a town keep pumps, valves, filters, seals, electronics, medical devices, greenhouse lights, rovers, and pressure vessels running for decades?  
  **Reality status:** Repair culture and additive manufacturing exist, but broad local manufacturing of mission-critical components is not proven on Mars.  
  **Writer decisions:** What can Sol fabricate? What must come from Ferrum or Earth? Which components are rationed? How powerful are maintenance workers and repair archives?

- [ ] **Medical care, pharmaceuticals, and evacuation limits**  
  **Question to invent:** How does a town handle trauma, surgery, infection, chronic disease, mental health, dental care, birth complications, and medicines when Earth evacuation is slow or impossible?  
  **Reality status:** Remote medicine and space medicine provide precedents, but a Mars town hospital is fictional extrapolation.  
  **Writer decisions:** What can Elena’s medbay actually treat? Which conditions require transfer to Elysium or Earth? What drugs can be manufactured locally? What does a medical flight cost—and who gets one?

- [ ] **Mars-born health, partial gravity, reproduction, and childhood**  
  **Question to invent:** Can people safely conceive, carry pregnancies, give birth, develop, age, and return to Earth after long exposure to 0.38g, radiation, isolation, and Mars-specific microbiology?  
  **Reality status:** This is profoundly unresolved. Human reproduction and lifelong development in partial gravity have not been demonstrated.  
  **Writer decisions:** This is a foundational canon choice. Are Mars-born people medically distinct? Is pregnancy regulated, celebrated, private, dangerous, or politically contested? Can Lina or Tomas ever comfortably live on Earth?

- [ ] **Settlement-scale computing, sensors, and cyber resilience**  
  **Question to invent:** How does Sol monitor life-support systems without becoming helpless when automation, sensors, networks, or software fail?  
  **Reality status:** Industrial control and spacecraft automation exist; cyber-secure, repairable town autonomy is an extrapolation.  
  **Writer decisions:** What can residents override manually? Who has access to life-support data? Are old systems understandable, or is the town dependent on black-box vendors?

# Priority 2 — The town must become durable and socially real

These make the setting believable over years and generations rather than merely technically alive.

- [ ] **Habitat construction, expansion, and end-of-life replacement**  
  **Question to invent:** How was Sol built, how does it add homes or public spaces, and what happens when founding-era pressure modules age out?  
  **Reality status:** Landing, robotics, construction, and regolith shielding have active research paths; a town-scale build/renewal industry is fictional extrapolation.  
  **Writer decisions:** Is Sol a chain of buried rings, a cut-and-cover town, a crater settlement, or a hybrid? Are original modules sacred heritage or dangerous liabilities? Who pays to replace them?

- [ ] **Local resources and industrial economy**  
  **Question to invent:** What does Sol extract, process, repair, design, export, or provide that lets it remain relevant after other cities outgrow it?  
  **Reality status:** Mars ISRU has been demonstrated in limited robotic form—for example, MOXIE made oxygen from the Martian atmosphere—but a full industrial economy has not been demonstrated.  
  **Writer decisions:** Is Sol economically dependent, subsidized as heritage, or specialized in agriculture, repair, archives, training, or a nearby resource? What does it trade with Ferrum, Meridian, Elysium, Astra, and Arcadia?

- [ ] **Transport inside Mars**  
  **Question to invent:** How do people, cargo, urgent patients, food, replacement parts, and tourists travel between settlements?  
  **Reality status:** No inter-city Martian transport network exists. Every route, vehicle, depot, fuel source, schedule, and rescue protocol is fictional.  
  **Writer decisions:** Pressure trains, hoppers, cargo crawlers, suborbital flights, or a mixed system? How long is Sol to Meridian? Who can afford travel? What does a delayed shuttle mean emotionally?

- [ ] **Earth–Mars logistics, launch windows, and strategic reserves**  
  **Question to invent:** What remains imported from Earth, how often, at what cost, with what delay, and how does the town prepare for a missed launch window?  
  **Reality status:** Mars transfers are constrained by orbital mechanics and long communication/travel delays. A settled Mars would still depend on an interplanetary supply chain for some time.  
  **Writer decisions:** Which imports are non-negotiable—chips, medicines, catalysts, genes, precision bearings? How many months/years of reserves does Sol hold? What does an Earth political crisis do to a Martian household?

- [ ] **Communications and information sovereignty**  
  **Question to invent:** How do Sol residents communicate with Earth and other settlements when Earth has significant light-time delay and local networks can fail?  
  **Reality status:** Earth–Mars delay is fundamental physics; robust local networks and governance are design choices.  
  **Writer decisions:** Is live Earth conversation impossible? Are messages asynchronous and ritualized? Who owns the network? What kinds of rumors spread locally when official information is delayed?

- [ ] **Law, ownership, labor rights, and civic legitimacy**  
  **Question to invent:** Who owns the habitat, air, water, land rights, extraction rights, data, homes, and infrastructure? Who has authority during a crisis?  
  **Reality status:** Space law exists but does not resolve the lived governance of mature Martian towns.  
  **Writer decisions:** Habitat Council powers, unions, corporate presence, citizenship, birthright, migration rules, debt, property, inheritance, crime, and democratic legitimacy.

- [ ] **Education, culture, religion, grief, and ordinary leisure**  
  **Question to invent:** How does a population born and raised under constraint become a culture rather than a workforce?  
  **Reality status:** Human social needs are established; the specific Martian forms are open fiction.  
  **Writer decisions:** School, games, dating, music, food rituals, language drift, worship, coming-of-age, funerals, festivals, local myths, civic holidays, and what “home” means to a Mars-born child.

- [ ] **Population management, migration, and demographic continuity**  
  **Question to invent:** How does a 2,000-person town avoid demographic fragility while respecting autonomy and family life?  
  **Reality status:** Small isolated populations face social, genetic, medical, and skills-distribution challenges; no Mars precedent exists.  
  **Writer decisions:** Immigration, partnerships between settlements, reproductive care, genetic archives, aging population, youth departure, adoption, and the social meaning of being Sol-born.

# Priority 3 — The town must have a future beyond survival

These decide why Habitat Sol is still alive and narratively important after Mars has cities.

- [ ] **Relationship to the larger Martian settlement network**  
  **Question to invent:** Why does a population-2,000 founding town remain inhabited when Meridian, Elysium, Ferrum, Astra, and Arcadia offer larger futures?  
  **Writer decisions:** Heritage status, specialized work, political autonomy, cheap housing, deep local family ties, a unique resource, institutional prestige, or a refusal to become a museum.

- [ ] **Tourism, heritage, and the commercialization of “firstness”**  
  **Question to invent:** Who profits from Habitat Sol being the first town on Mars, and what does that do to residents?  
  **Writer decisions:** Founding-day festivals, archive tours, expensive visitor permits, preservation law, tourists who treat Sol as a set, and residents who resent or need them.

- [ ] **Environmental ethics and planetary protection after settlement**  
  **Question to invent:** How does a mature Mars society treat potential native life, contaminated regions, terraforming claims, mining scars, and protected landscapes?  
  **Writer decisions:** Conservation zones, scientific conflict, local versus Earth authority, sacred sites, and whether humans have the right to permanently transform Mars.

- [ ] **A believable path to greater self-sufficiency**  
  **Question to invent:** What does “self-sufficient” actually mean for Sol, and what dependencies remain acceptable?  
  **Writer decisions:** Choose a target: (a) days of independence, (b) one missed supply window, (c) years without Earth cargo, or (d) true industrial independence. Do not treat these as the same thing.

# Suggested order for early canon workshops

1. Air/pressure and failure containment.
2. Water source and water politics.
3. Power architecture and dust season.
4. Radiation shielding and the physical form of Sol.
5. Food, fertilizer, and the greenhouse economy.
6. Mars-born health, reproduction, and return-to-Earth limits.
7. Transport/resupply and why Sol is still inhabited.
8. Governance, labor, and ownership of life-support infrastructure.

# Starting sources

These sources ground the checklist. They support constraints and components; they do not prove Habitat Sol can exist at town scale.

1. NASA, [Mars Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/) — atmosphere, climate, seasons, and broad environmental context.
2. NASA, [The Fact and Fiction of Martian Dust Storms](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/the-fact-and-fiction-of-martian-dust-storms/) — operational dust and solar-power risk.
3. NASA, [Environmental Control and Life Support Systems](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210010644/downloads/132-1-Final.pdf) (2021) — air revitalization, water recovery, waste, and exploration-system drivers.
4. NASA, [Environmental Control and Life Support System](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/g-657270-59-hp-environmental-control-and-life-support-system-eclss.pdf) — ISS operational overview.
5. NASA, [Growing Plants in Space](https://www.nasa.gov/exploration-research-and-technology/growing-plants-in-space/) — plant systems, root-zone management, hygiene, automation, and research limits.
6. NASA, [Advanced Plant Habitat](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/advanced-plant-habitat/) — controlled plant-bioscience research hardware.
7. NASA/JPL, [MOXIE](https://mars.nasa.gov/mars2020/spacecraft/instruments/moxie/) — robotic oxygen-production technology demonstration on Mars; not a settlement-scale industrial system.
8. NASA, [Mars Exploration Science Goals](https://science.nasa.gov/planetary-science/programs/mars-exploration/science-goals/) — Mars environment and human-exploration context.
