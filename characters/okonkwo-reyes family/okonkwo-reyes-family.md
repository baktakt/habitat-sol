# The Okonkwo-Reyes Family

The Okonkwo-Reyes family are one of the two core neighboring families in Habitat Sol. They are the civic/medical household next door to the Varga-Satos: more public-facing, more fluent in institutional language, and more exposed to the ethical compromises of keeping a small Martian town alive.

## Family identity

The household is built around two kinds of care:

- **Amara** tries to keep the town politically and morally coherent.
- **Elena** tries to keep bodies alive inside an environment that never wanted them.
- **Nico** tests whether adults mean what they say about permanence and safety.
- **Safi** treats Mars as ordinary and Earth as a storybook planet.

Their home should feel warm but compressed: civic documents on a wall screen, medical bags near the door, children's drawings in unlikely places, and old Sol repair labels layered over newer Elysium-made equipment.

## Image-generation rules for this family

Use documentary portraiture rather than fashion/editorial polish. Clothing is practical, modest, repaired, and habitat-specific. Patches and badges may appear as **abstract insignia only**; do not rely on generated readable text. Children must be age-appropriate and non-sexualized.

Shared family palette:

- ochre, umber, olive, faded council blue, clinic white, Mars red scarf/trim;
- worn canvas, thermal knits, polymer utility panels, soft indoor shoes;
- warm brown skin tones and tired eyes in practical light;
- backgrounds: council corridors, compact clinic rooms, school passages, greenhouse-adjacent domestic spaces.

| Public face | trusted civic voice; the person residents ask to make conflict bearable |
| Private burden | believes Sol may be losing the ethics that made settlement possible |
| Core contradiction | can calm any room except her own fear that compromise has become complicity |

Amara grew up on stories of the founding generation: ration votes, airlock funerals, repair shifts where everyone knew whose children were sleeping behind which pressure door. Her parents were not famous founders, but they were the kind of ordinary citizens Sol mythologizes: committee volunteers, emergency cooks, people who knew the old maintenance maps by memory. Amara inherited the idea that a habitat is a moral agreement before it is an engineering object.

Before joining the council she worked crisis negotiation: domestic disputes during rationing, labor walkouts in life-support-adjacent departments, evacuation triage after small pressure incidents. She is good at finding the sentence that lets an angry person remain dignified. That gift made her beloved, then indispensable, then tired.

With Elena, Amara is tender but stubborn. She knows Elena is right that Sol nostalgia can become dangerous, yet she cannot abandon the civic story that raised her. With Nico she is careful not to confuse gratitude with obedience. With Safi she is playful in a way few residents see.

Elena trained in Habitat Elysium, where medical systems are newer, cleaner, and less sentimental. She came to Sol after the decompression accident that killed her previous partner; officially for a difficult specialist post, privately because Sol was far enough from the version of her life she had imagined.

She understands pregnancy, childhood development, bone density, immune drift, endocrine changes, grief, and all the small ways Mars edits human bodies. She is not cold, but she is allergic to romantic language about sacrifice when the clinic is under-supplied. She loves Amara partly because Amara still believes public life can be honorable; she argues with Amara because belief alone does not sterilize instruments or stabilize calcium loss.

Elena's relationship with Nico is careful: she refuses to turn his past into a diagnostic file. With Safi she is softer than she intends to be, especially when Safi asks impossible questions about Earth animals.

| Temperament | smart, wary, observant |
| Hook | fascinated by forbidden exterior zones and the town's hidden infrastructure |
| Core contradiction | wants to belong without owing anyone a performance of gratitude |

Nico arrived in Sol after an evacuation from a smaller mining settlement. The official records are partial, delayed, or sealed; whether this is bureaucracy, trauma protection, or something more political remains an open question. Nico remembers enough to distrust clean stories. He has learned how adults change tone when they reach the part they do not want to explain.

He is observant in the way of children who have had to map exits. He notices which doors are locked, which vents sound wrong, which adults are lying out of kindness. He is good with old habitat diagrams and drawn to infrastructure that has been declared off-limits. With Lina he shares a hunger for elsewhere, though Nico's elsewhere is less glamorous: sealed service tunnels, forgotten pressure zones, places where the town's real history might be stored.

Amara and Elena try not to overprotect him. He interprets that restraint as respect on good days and abandonment on bad ones.

### Safi Okonkwo-Reyes

| Origin | born in Habitat Sol |
| Temperament | cheerful, strange, imaginative |
| Hook | draws Earth animals she has never seen |
| Core contradiction | understands Mars perfectly and Earth incorrectly |

Safi has never experienced open sky as anything but a dangerous idea. She knows the names of pressure doors, algae tanks, pollination schedules, and which adults keep sweets in which pockets. Earth animals fascinate her because they sound invented: whales, horses, pigeons, dogs that breathe outside.

She draws animals from descriptions and gets them wrong in revealing ways: birds with pressure masks, cows with radiator fins, rain falling upward because condensation does. Adults find her drawings funny until they realize she is building a mythology of Earth from their nostalgia.

Safi is not naive about danger; she simply does not treat danger as unusual. Her cheerfulness should have a slightly uncanny Martian normality.

## Family dynamics

- Amara is rooted in Sol's civic identity and fears civic language becoming propaganda.
- Elena loves Amara but does not always love Sol; she trusts bodies and evidence before myths.
- Nico is still deciding whether permanence is real or just something adults say when they need children to sleep.
- Safi treats Mars as normal and Earth as myth.
- Amara and Elena's arguments are rarely about facts; they are about what people owe a place that keeps them alive.
- Nico and Safi are siblings by adoption/birth but share the same pressure doors, dinner table, and emergency drills.

- Amara mediating a water policy dispute where every side is partly right,

- Nico and Lina discovering a closed section of the habitat,
- Safi drawing rain incorrectly,
- Elena receiving an offer from Elysium,
- Amara defending Sol against heritage-tourism plans,
- Nico finding an evacuation record that contradicts what he was told,
- Safi asking whether Earth animals would be allowed through quarantine.
