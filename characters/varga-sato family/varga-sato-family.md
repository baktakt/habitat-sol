# The Varga-Sato Family

The Varga-Sato family are one of the two core neighboring families in Habitat Sol. They are the greenhouse/maintenance household next door to the Okonkwo-Reyes family: less publicly ideological, more physically entangled with the systems that keep Sol breathing, fed, warm, repaired, and afraid.

## Family identity

The household is built around practical love:

- **Mira** protects people by understanding fragile systems before they fail.
- **Kenji** protects people by repairing what everyone else has already cursed at.
- **Lina** wants a life large enough to escape Sol's inherited stories.
- **Tomas** loves machines and corridors as if the habitat itself were alive.

Their home should feel like a family kitchen attached to a workshop: plant cuttings in improvised containers, rover parts on a side table, greenhouse schedules, school notices, old tools, and the constant sense that someone may be called away mid-meal.

## Image-generation rules for this family

Use municipal documentary realism and practical intimacy. This family should not look like military astronauts or sleek sci-fi heroes. Their clothes are workwear, schoolwear, thermal layers, and repaired domestic garments. Patches and labels may appear as **abstract insignia only**; avoid readable generated text.

Shared family palette:

- greenhouse green, algae-tank teal, orange exterior workwear, faded ochre, graphite, rust red;
- worn canvas, utility straps, patched sleeves, soft indoor layers;
- backgrounds: greenhouse rows, repair bays, airlock staging rooms, family workshop corners, corridors with departure boards blurred in the distance.

Children and teenagers must be age-appropriate and non-sexualized. Lina is sixteen: restless and stylish in a teenage way, not adult glamour. Tomas is nine: school-age, not a toddler.

| Private fear | agricultural systems are less stable than officials admit |
| Core contradiction | feeds the town while withholding how close the margins really are |

Mira remembers Earth mostly through body memory: humidity, soil smell, a childhood sky that feels too large to describe without sounding religious. She moved to Mars young enough that Sol shaped her adult nervous system, but old enough to know plants once grew without committee meetings.

As greenhouse systems lead she is responsible for the part of Sol that most resembles hope. Residents visit the greenhouses to feel reassured; Mira sees fungal pressure, root stress, nutrient variance, brittle supply chains, and the small lies needed to keep panic away. She is trusted because she does not dramatize danger. She is dangerous to officials because she knows when calm language has become concealment.

With Kenji she shares a repair-person's marriage: love expressed as pre-packed tools, saved leftovers, and knowing when not to ask. With Lina she is afraid of becoming the obstacle her daughter needs to push against. With Tomas she is gentler than she realizes.

- Woman in early 40s; dark curly hair tied back or escaping a practical tie.

- Practical green/olive utility jumpsuit or greenhouse jacket, patched knees/cuffs, sleeves rolled.
- Abstract greenhouse patch, seed tag, sensor tablet, pruning tool, or nutrient-stained gloves.
- Backgrounds: greenhouse aisle, condensation on panels, plant light, irrigation hardware, red exterior glow through glass.
- Avoid fashion styling; she should look competent before she looks beautiful.

Kenji was born somewhere that already treated Earth as a destination rather than a home. Lunar orbit, a transfer habitat, or another off-Earth environment remains deliberately open canon, but he grew up with the idea that bodies are shaped by gravity bargains. He came to Sol through machines: rovers, seals, exterior manipulators, thermal loops, the ugly practical devices that make heroism unnecessary.

He is beloved because he makes frightening systems feel approachable. He explains malfunctions with jokes, teaches children the names of tools, and can tell from a motor's vibration whether a repair will take ten minutes or ruin the day. His hands are scarred, dry, and careful. He keeps obsolete tools that belonged to the first repair crews.

His private longing is Earth. Not the ideological Earth in Safi's drawings or the commercial Earth of travel feeds, but a physical planet he may no longer tolerate. He hides medical concerns because he does not want longing turned into pity.

Lina has grown up surrounded by adults who call Sol historic when they mean limited. She loves her parents, knows the smell of greenhouse corridors, can identify rover faults by the tension in Kenji's shoulders, and still wants a bigger life than the town seems willing to offer. She hates wearing the Sol patch and often tears it from her clothes, leaving the outline and loose threads behind.

Her imagination is fed by metropolitan media streams from Astra, Meridian, and Elysium: cleaner clothes, wider corridors, competitive schools, art scenes, bodies moving through public space without everyone knowing their parents. She adapts those styles using what Sol can provide: customized jacket cuffs, a repurposed strap, a bright lining, a small unauthorized sticker on a school tablet. Her rebellion is visual before it is political.

She is not shallow. She is furious that adults mistake ambition for betrayal. With Nico, she can say things she would never say at family breakfast. Their shared interest in sealed spaces begins as boredom and becomes a route into Sol's buried history.

| Core contradiction | understands the habitat as a living companion while adults insist it is only infrastructure |

Tomas is old enough to move through Sol with small permitted freedoms and young enough that adults underestimate what he remembers. He likes repair bays because machines are honest: broken things do not pretend to be fine. He names small maintenance robots, apologizes to stuck doors, and notices when a ventilation sound changes pitch.

He is not a prodigy in a glossy genius-child way. He is attentive. He remembers where Kenji leaves old washers, which greenhouse door Mira checks twice, and which corridor lights flicker before a system warning. Adults call this imagination until it becomes useful.

Tomas should look like a nine-year-old school-age child, not a toddler and not a tiny astronaut. His relationship to tools is domestic and curious, not heroic.

## Family dynamics

- Mira protects through competence and withheld worry.
- Kenji protects through humor and repair.
- Lina wants a larger life than Sol seems to offer and hates when adults romanticize limitation.
- Tomas loves the habitat as if it were alive.
- Mira and Kenji know Lina may need to leave; they disagree silently about whether leaving means success or loss.
- Lina treats Tomas as annoying until he notices something she missed.
- Kenji teaches Tomas tools; Mira teaches Tomas systems; Lina teaches Tomas how to keep secrets badly.
