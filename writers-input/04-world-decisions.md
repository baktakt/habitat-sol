## Decision: Travel between settlements

**Status:** provisional

**Why it matters**

Determines how separation, migration, work, family visits, and leaving Sol function emotionally.

**Chosen direction**

Travel is routine for some professionals but expensive and emotionally significant for ordinary residents.

**Consequences**

- leaving Sol is achievable but not casual;
- teenagers can realistically dream of Elysium;
- families may remain separated for long periods;
- transport interruptions can produce intimate stories.

**Still open**

- exact prices;
- vehicle models;
- journey durations;
- booking systems.

## Decision: Earth oceans through Mars-born eyes

**Status:** provisional

**Why it matters**

Earth nostalgia and Mars identity should not be assumed to mean the same thing across generations.

**Chosen direction**

Some people born on Mars may find Earth oceans disturbing rather than beautiful.

**Consequences**

- Earth imagery can evoke unease as well as longing;
- a Mars-born character may experience open water as alien, excessive, or unsafe.

**Still open**

- how common this response is;
- which stories, if any, make it specific to a character.

## Priority world systems

Only define these when a story needs them:

- why people remain in Sol;
- movement between settlements;
- work, ownership, and social class;
- food, water, air, and housing;
- childhood and family life;
- communication with Earth;
- death, burial, and remembrance;
- how Mars-born people view Earth;
- political tension between Sol and newer cities;
- what technology can and cannot conveniently solve.
